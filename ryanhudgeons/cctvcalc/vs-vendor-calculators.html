CCTV Calc vs. camera vendor calculators

Short answer: a vendor calculator uses that vendor's own bitrate tables. CCTV Calc estimates a typical bitrate from resolution, codec, frame rate and scene, so the two can differ by 10–30% either way. When you type in the camera's configured bitrate, CCTV Calc uses your number, and the storage math matches.

The estimate CCTV Calc uses

Unless you enter a configured bitrate, each stream starts from a reference point of 4,000 kbps for 1080p (2.07 MP) H.264 at 30 fps in a typical scene, and scales with pixel count to the power 0.8, frame rate, codec and scene. That reproduces the common vendor tables: about 2 Mbps at 1 MP, 6 Mbps at 4 MP and 12 Mbps at 8 MP.

kbps = 4000 × (MP ÷ 2.0736)^0.8 × (fps ÷ 30) × codec × scene

Codec factors: H.264 1.00, H.264+ 0.60, H.265 0.55, H.265+ 0.30. Scene factors: low (indoor, static) 0.7, medium 1.0, high (traffic, foliage, night noise) 1.4. A busy scene at night is where a vendor's typical figure and a real camera drift apart the most.

required TB = kbps × 1000 ÷ 8 × 86,400 × duty × retention days ÷ 10¹²

How to make the two match

Open the camera's stream settings, read the configured bitrate (or the average from the NVR's live view), and enter it in the group's Configured bitrate field. CCTV Calc then uses your number instead of the estimate, and the result is the same arithmetic any calculator should do. If the numbers still differ, compare units: TB on the drive label, TiB in the NVR menu.
